Commands

Client-side, so they work on any server. Chat suggests song titles; a partial title works if only one song matches.

Command Does
/cmm play <song> Plays the song now, then the queue, then the game's music.
/cmm play Resumes.
/cmm queue <song> Adds the song to the queue.
/cmm next Next song.
/cmm prev Previous song, or the start of this one.
/cmm pause Pauses.
/cmm repeat [off|all|one] Sets repeat, or cycles it with no argument.
/cmm dislike [song] Dislikes the song, or the one playing. Again to take the dislike away.

Mixing

With an add-on that adds songs, a Mix chip shows a list as a mix. Click a transition to edit it: scroll sideways over a song, or drag it, to move it a beat at a time. The pack below is made up, with made-up tempos, keys and waveforms, so it's silent.

The interactive player needs JavaScript.

Auto picks as a DJ would: an 8-bar Blend when the tempos are within 6% (or double or half) and the keys go together, a Rise when the tempos match but the keys clash, a Fade for a small tempo gap, and a short Echo for a big one. It ends where the first song's music fades, not in its quiet tail, and starts on a bar so both songs' bars line up. Beatmatching speeds the next song up or down by up to 8%, without changing its pitch, then glides back over 8 seconds. Tempo and key are worked out from the audio and can be off, such as half or double the tempo.

The queue

The interactive player needs JavaScript.

Previous restarts the song after its first 3 seconds.

After that, the game's own music returns, and the game still decides when: a random break of up to 20 minutes on the default Music Frequency, and different music on the title screen, underwater, in the Nether, and so on. The queue shows the game's next song and its break; click it to play it now.

Songs the game starts play exactly as in vanilla, apart from disliked songs: the same weighted pick from sounds.json (a disliked song is drawn again, so the rest keep their odds; if every song for the situation is disliked, the game stays quiet until its next break), the same break, each file's own volume and pitch, and the same "Now playing" toast. They still stop when you change dimension or when music that replaces them starts, such as the dragon fight. Songs you pick or queue yourself carry on through dimension changes and menus, and while a song is paused the game's music stays quiet.

Resource packs

A pack that adds or replaces music gets its own page. The album is the pack's name, the art is its icon (or its initials on a gradient if it has none), and the artist is the "By ..." line of its description, or Unknown artist.

A pack can name its songs itself with assets/<namespace>/cuemymusic.json. Keys are sound files as in sounds.json, and any field left out falls back to the above:

{
  "tracks": {
    "mypack:music/rain": { "title": "Rain", "artist": "Someone", "album": "Weather" }
  }
}

The player lists every sound event whose name starts with music.. Every song a pack adds is an add-on song: Add-ons mode plays them in place of the game's music, and Vanilla + FX leaves them out, even ones a pack puts in the game's own events.
