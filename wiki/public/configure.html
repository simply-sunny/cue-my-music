Configure

Open the player and click Settings. Drag the sliders and EQ bands, flip the switches and pick a preset below; with a song playing, a ring by the value shows the change on its way to the speakers. In the game that takes a few seconds, the audio already queued. Game songs play 30-second previews; pitch and EQ aren't heard here.

The interactive player needs JavaScript.

What the screen leaves out:

Setting Notes
Pause during travel Your song fades out as a portal takes you, waits while the new dimension loads, then fades back in from the same spot. Joining or leaving a world never stops it.
Crossfade Game songs crossfade too. Songs only crossfade at the same sounds.json volume and sample rate, playing in the same place; otherwise they play one after the other. Songs automix blends use their own transition.
Automix add-on songs On. Shown with an add-on page. Add-on songs play at the same loudness, without silence at their ends, and blend into the next (see Mixing). They need the same sample rate to blend.
Keep add-on songs out of recordings On. Shown with an add-on page. Add-on songs play from a small separate Java process while the game plays silence, so recorders of the game's own audio (rec-util, OBS application capture) leave them out. Recorders of all system audio still pick them up. Applies from the next song.

Settings, liked and disliked songs, and playlists are saved to config/cuemymusic.json in your Minecraft folder:

  • Windows: %appdata%\.minecraft\config\cuemymusic.json
  • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/minecraft/config/cuemymusic.json
  • Linux: ~/.minecraft/config/cuemymusic.json

Tempo, key, loudness and waveforms of add-on songs are worked out once per song and saved in config/cuemymusic-analysis/, so they're ready straight away next time. A song is worked out again if its file changes. Deleting the folder is safe.

Delete the file to reset to defaults. One value goes further in the JSON than in the screen. Edit it, then restart:

Key Default Notes
speed 1.0 Any value from 0.5 to 2.0, to two decimals, such as 1.08.
